THE COMPLETE WALKTHROUGH · PAGE BY PAGE

Know what every part does—and how to use it.

This manual explains the purpose, controls, expected results, and practical use of each Life OS workspace. It is written for the person using the website, not for developers or administrators.

19 chapters·Practical examples·Desktop and mobile
Important distinction

The home page and both manuals are public reading pages. Diary, planning, progress, and account-management workspaces are behind sign-in. Links in this manual open the real workspace; when signed out, you will be asked to sign in first.

01 · HOW THE SYSTEM FITS TOGETHER

Five kinds of information, one connected plan

Vision 30 is a personal planning and reflection workspace. It helps connect a broad direction to a practical action and a later review. It does not decide what your priorities should be or verify that a result has happened; you enter and assess your own records.

CONTEXT

Vision and life areas

The Vision 30 and Life pages describe the wider direction. Eight pillars help organize different areas of development.

PLAN

Blocks, stages, goals

Blocks and stages organize work over time. Goals describe outcomes; targets and milestones can make selected outcomes easier to observe.

DO

Tasks and habits

Tasks are actions you can finish. Habits are repeatable practices you can check off for a date.

LEARN

Check-ins and reviews

Daily records and periodic reviews help capture what happened, what evidence exists, and what you want to change.

A useful example

Direction: improve physical health. Goal: complete a consistent walking routine by a chosen date. Task: take a 25-minute walk today. Habit: walk on planned days. Evidence: record completed walks or another meaningful measure. Review: decide whether the routine is realistic and helping.

These records are related by the choices you make in their forms. Creating a task does not automatically create a goal, and completing a task does not automatically mark a goal or target achieved.

02 · FIRST SIGN-IN

Enter the private workspace safely

  1. Open Home, choose Sign in, or open the login page directly.
  2. Enter the username and password provided for your account. There is no public sign-up or password-reset workflow on the login page.
  3. After successful sign-in, the site returns you to the page you initially requested when possible. If you used the login link directly, go to Today.
  4. On your first visit, read the current plan before editing it. If the baseline framework has not been seeded by the site owner, ask them to initialize it rather than attempting to create a new account or database records yourself.
  5. Use Settings to download a backup after you have entered information worth keeping.
Account safety

Do not share your password, ask someone to place it in a shared file, or put it in a journal entry. The public home and manuals do not reveal your private records; still sign out when using a device that other people can access.

03 · MOVING AROUND

Find a page and understand the shared controls

Once signed in, the sidebar lists the main workspaces. On a narrow screen, open the Navigation disclosure near the top of the page to see the same links. The brand at the top returns to Today. The page header includes shortcuts to Home, the User guide, backup export, and Settings.

Home
Public introduction and direct links to the private areas. It does not contain diary data.
User guide
The short user guide. Its Detailed manual link opens this longer page-by-page reference.
Messages
After a form is submitted, a message near the top reports whether it saved or needs correction. Read it before repeating an action.
Sign out
The sidebar submits a sign-out request. Sign out when you finish on a shared or borrowed device.
Manage records
Opens Django admin, an administrative interface for authorized accounts. It is not needed for ordinary daily use.

Most create, update, toggle, import, and delete actions submit a form and reload the page with a confirmation or error. Use the visible button once, wait for the response, and avoid resubmitting repeatedly if the result is unclear.

04 · DAILY WORKSPACE

Today — decide what matters now

Today is the day-to-day command center. The summary cards show the day number in the configured Vision 30 date window, days until the master deadline, the active block, and an execution consistency snapshot when there is recorded work.

Top priorities

Add up to three focus items, choosing a rank from 1 to 3 and writing a short title. Saving a priority at a rank already used for today replaces that rank's title. Use the round control beside an item to mark it complete or not complete.

Example

Rank 1: “Send the reviewed project proposal.” Rank 2: “Walk for 25 minutes.” Rank 3: “Review this week's expenses.” These are outcomes for today; break them into smaller tasks if needed.

Tasks due and overdue

Tasks with today's due date appear in the daily list. Use the completion button to mark a task complete or reopen it. Overdue incomplete tasks are called out so you can decide whether to reschedule, do, or remove the commitment on the Tasks page.

Eight-pillar check-in

For each active pillar, select Not completed, Partially completed, or Completed. The reflection field is an optional short note; outcome evidence is a separate optional note. Select a stage link to open that pillar's staged plan. Save each pillar's form separately.

Example

Physical practice: “Partially completed.” Brief note: “Walked for 12 minutes before rain.” Outcome evidence: “Three walks this week.” The first note describes today's effort; the evidence summarizes a result to consider.

Habits and evening reflection

Habit controls record or remove today's check-in. The evening reflection form can record mood, energy (when enabled), wins, an improvement, and a free-form reflection. When you save it, the app records an execution snapshot based on the plans and check-ins it can measure. It is not an outcome score or a judgment of your day.

Good daily use: choose a few priorities in the morning, update actions as they happen, and close the day with only the notes you will find useful later. A blank day is allowed.

05 · SUMMARY WORKSPACE

Dashboard — see the current system at a glance

The Dashboard summarizes the framework and active work: number of configured blocks, the eight pillars, open tasks, and explicitly created targets. Its current block card links to the block workspace; its master timeline card links to the Vision page. The lower panels show next actions and outcome targets when present.

Use the Dashboard to orient yourself or notice where the plan is empty. It does not replace Today, and it does not invent example tasks or targets to make the page look full.

Example

If the Dashboard says there are no measurable targets, open the relevant block or Goals & Tasks and define a meaningful measure only if one is useful. Do not create a numeric target just to fill the summary card.

The separate /overview/ route is an alias to the Dashboard. The primary navigation uses the Dashboard label.

06 · DIRECTION AND DATES

Vision 30 — understand the full horizon

The Vision page presents the saved master vision, master start and deadline, inclusive total-day count, remaining days, and a timeline of the blocks. It distinguishes pre-block setup time, planned block time, and the period after the last block. The master deadline and block dates are related but are not automatically stretched to match.

Each timeline row links to its block workspace. The permanent pillars are also linked to their stage plans. Use this page for context; edit the master dates or vision in Settings, and edit an individual block from Blocks.

Date counting

The project's planned blocks count the start date as Day 01. Date changes in Settings do not automatically recompute the saved block dates, so review the timeline and block schedule after changing the master window.

07 · TIME-BOUND PLANNING

Blocks and block workspaces

The Blocks page lists the configured periods with their number, name, dates, status, duration, mission labels, target count, and milestone count. Choose Open workspace to see or edit one block. Blocks are displayed with a 100-day plan in the seeded framework, but the saved start and deadline determine the actual duration.

Block plan fields

The block form holds its name, start and deadline, vision, mission, objectives, weekly targets, monthly milestones, definition of done, status, final review, lessons, and recommendations for the next step. Save the block plan to submit those edits. The end date must not be before the start date.

Pillar missions

Each block can contain a mission for each pillar and a linked stage. Expand a mission to read its current description and edit the mission, objectives, daily actions, weekly targets, monthly milestones, definition of done, status, and review. The block list's seed data provides a framework; personalize it to make it actionable.

Example

For a Business & professional mission: “Publish the new service page and review qualified enquiries.” Daily action: “Spend one focused session on the page.” Weekly target: “Review enquiries each Friday.” Definition of done: “Page is live, links are checked, and enquiry results are reviewed.”

Targets and milestones

A measurable target is an outcome measure with a title, optional block/pillar/stage, unit, target and current values, due date, completion criteria, evidence, and status. Target values must be positive when supplied; current values cannot be negative. Progress percentage is calculated when the target is measurable, but status is not changed automatically. Add a milestone for a dated checkpoint; its Complete/Reopen control records or clears its completion date.

Use the block review link to evaluate evidence for the period. A milestone, task, target, and mission are different records and are not automatically completed together.

08 · AREAS AND LEARNING PATHS

Pillars and pillar detail pages

The Pillars page presents the eight permanent life domains and ten stages per domain. Each tile displays its description, measurement approach, and stage shortcuts. Choose Open to work with a pillar in more detail.

  • Mental
  • Physical
  • Spiritual
  • Financial
  • Personal
  • Character
  • Business & professional
  • Family

Measurement approach

A pillar can use qualitative evidence, numeric targets specific to that pillar, practice consistency, or a custom method. The detail page lets you choose the method and add notes explaining how to interpret it. This setting describes the approach; it does not calculate a universal score.

Stages and stage reviews

Expand a stage to edit its title, vision, mission, syllabus, resources, objectives, daily practices, weekly and monthly milestones, completion criteria, evidence, and optional completion date. The stage review form stores a dated evaluation, evidence, lessons, and recommendations as history. It also updates the stage evidence text when evidence is submitted. A stage should only be marked complete after reviewing its criteria and evidence.

The pillar detail page also lists the block missions and measurable targets connected to that pillar. Use those links to move between a staged development plan and its time-bound work.

Example

For a Mental stage, set a practical learning objective, add a resource you actually intend to use, and define completion evidence such as a finished project or written summary. At the review, record what was completed, the evidence, one lesson, and the next recommendation.

09 · OUTCOMES AND ACTIONS

Goals & Tasks — connect the result to the next step

This combined page has separate forms for goals and tasks and separate lists for each. A goal describes an outcome that may need multiple actions. A task describes one next action. Create either item here; created tasks also appear on the Tasks page and, when due today, on Today.

Creating a goal

Use a concise title and description. You may connect the goal to a pillar and block, define a numeric target and current value with a unit, set completion criteria, record evidence, choose a deadline, and enter a completion date once the criteria are met. Target values must be greater than zero; current progress cannot be negative. Leave numeric fields blank if a numeric measure is inappropriate.

Example goal

Title: “Build a repeatable weekly client outreach process.” Pillar: Business & professional. Block: the block where you will work on it. Completion criteria: “Process documented, used for four weeks, and results reviewed.” Evidence: add a short note when you have genuine results. A completed task alone does not complete this goal.

Updating a goal

Expand the goal and choose Update goal to save a new current value, evidence, and—only after verifying criteria—a completion date. Other fields are retained from the goal form. The displayed percentage is derived from current value divided by target when both are available. It is a progress indicator, not verification of completion.

Connecting next actions

When creating a task, optionally associate it with the relevant goal, block, and pillar. For example, “Draft outreach email template” can be a task supporting the outreach-process goal. Keep the task specific enough to finish and use the due date only when it is a real planning aid.

The list provides a completion toggle and a delete action. Deleting a goal or task removes that record; there is no undo control on the page. Export a backup before substantial cleanup if you may need to restore the data.

10 · ACTION MANAGEMENT

Tasks — keep next actions concrete

The Tasks page has an add form and an all-tasks list. A task includes title, description, status, priority, optional due date, and optional pillar, block, and goal associations. The task list displays its status, priority, due date, and relevant associations.

Vague versus useful

Vague: “Business.” More actionable: “Review the three open client proposals and send the next response.” A useful task tells you what to do when you return to the list.

Use the round check button to switch between completed and not-completed status. The current page offers status toggle and delete controls rather than an inline edit form for an existing task; create carefully, and use the Django admin only if you need to edit task fields after saving. On Today, a task due on that date appears in the due-today list. An incomplete task with a past due date is highlighted as overdue.

Before deleting an item, decide whether rescheduling or changing its status would better preserve its history. The delete action permanently removes the task from the app's active records.

11 · REPEATABLE PRACTICE

Habits — record a practice without inventing a streak

Create a habit with a title, optional description, optional pillar, and active setting. The list shows whether it is active, its recorded check-in count, and whether it has been checked off today. A new habit is not automatically checked or assigned a streak.

Press the round check button to add today's check-in. Press it again to remove today's check-in. Today also displays active habits so you can record them from the daily workspace. Delete removes the habit and its related check-ins; there is no recovery action in the page.

Example

Habit: “Read for 20 minutes.” Description: “Read the current course material.” Pillar: Mental. Mark it after completing the practice. If the day was only partly completed, use the pillar check-in to describe partial effort; the habit itself is a simple yes/no check-in.

Use habits for repeated behaviors, not one-off projects. A streak is only a pattern of saved dates; it is not shown as proof of character or long-term outcome.

12 · BIG-PICTURE CONTEXT

Life — return to the whole-life direction

The Life page brings together the current master vision, its date window, links to the full timeline and eight life domains, and a compact overview of pillars and their measurement methods. Choose a pillar's stage link to open its ten-stage plan.

This page is primarily for context and navigation. Edit the master vision or dates in Settings, edit block details in Blocks, and edit a pillar's learning path on that pillar's detail page.

Avoid false precision

The app intentionally distinguishes actions from outcomes. A completed task demonstrates that an action was recorded as done; it does not by itself prove improvement in a life domain.

13 · PERIODIC REFLECTION

Reviews — learn from a period, then choose a response

Use the tabs to choose Weekly, Monthly, or Block review. Weekly and monthly forms start with the current calendar period; the block review uses the active block when one exists. Check the period dates and selected block before saving, especially if you are writing a retrospective review.

The form provides structured fields for the period and block, eight-pillar performance, target progress, wins, problems, missed commitments, corrective actions, trends, achievements, risks, next milestones, target evaluation, evidence, completed and incomplete missions, lessons, next recommendations, execution consistency, and outcome progress. Fill the sections that help you think clearly; distinguish observations from guesses.

Example weekly review

Win: “Published the first service-page draft.” Problem: “Two planned writing sessions were displaced.” Evidence: “Draft link and enquiry count.” Lesson: “Writing earlier in the day was more reliable.” Next step: “Schedule two morning sessions next week.”

Saved reviews appear in the review history. The adjacent panel shows recent daily reflections; use Today for a daily entry. Stage reviews are recorded from the relevant pillar stage, not from the general Reviews form.

Reviews are saved as records, not automatically merged into each other or used to mark goals, targets, missions, or blocks complete. Review the relevant record and update it deliberately.

14 · PRIVATE NOTES

Journal — keep dated reflections and decisions

The Journal page lets you enter a date, optional title, mood, reflection text, optional decision, and optional pillar. New entries default to the current local date. Entries are displayed with their date and pillar, newest first.

Example entry

Title: “A better start to the day.” Mood: choose only if useful. Text: “I finished the proposal review before opening messages. It felt easier to focus.” Decision: “Keep the first work block free of notifications tomorrow.” Pillar: optional.

Journal entries are private personal records in the site's database and are included in the JSON backup. They can be deleted from their entry; that action has no in-page undo. Keep sensitive information to a minimum and store exported backups securely.

Today also has a shorter evening reflection with wins and an improvement prompt. Use the Journal for a fuller dated note; use Today to capture a concise day-close reflection.

15 · MEASUREMENT AND HISTORY

Progress — interpret evidence in context

The Progress page groups measurable targets by pillar, lists goal outcomes, compares available execution consistency averages for the last seven days and the previous seven days, and displays recent saved daily execution scores plus review and daily-reflection records.

Outcome measures

Targets retain their own unit and status. If current and target values are available, the page can show a percentage; targets with insufficient values display as not measured. Goals use their own current/target values and completion criteria. Use the progress display as a prompt to inspect evidence, not as an automatic declaration of success.

Execution consistency

The trend compares saved daily execution scores, not every calendar day. Days without a saved score are omitted rather than assumed to be zero. Daily execution snapshots are written when you save a daily reflection with measurable planned items. Review the underlying records before drawing conclusions from averages.

Use two questions

Execution: “Did I carry out the actions I planned?” Outcome: “What changed, and what evidence supports that?” The answers can differ; both are useful.

16 · PRESERVING HISTORY

Archive — keep records worth revisiting

The Archive page combines explicitly completed or archived blocks, saved period and stage reviews, journal history, and manually created archive records. It does not automatically move completed tasks to the archive.

Create an archive record with its date, type, title, text, and optional pillar. Use it for a meaningful achievement, a decision you want to remember, a life event, or other historical note that does not fit a normal task.

Example record

Kind: choose the closest available category. Title: “Completed first paid project using the new process.” Date: the relevant date. Text: note what happened and why it matters. Pillar: Business & professional, if useful.

To have a block appear in the completed-block section, update its status through the block workspace after reviewing it. Add a final review, lessons, and next recommendations there. Archive entries can be deleted from the page; block and review records are preserved in their own workspaces.

17 · PREFERENCES AND DATA PORTABILITY

Settings — adjust the framework and protect your data

Dates and preferences

The settings form can change the system name, master start, master deadline, first block start, master vision, timezone name, mood/energy option, and theme. The date and timezone fields are validated. The saved timezone affects how the app interprets local dates. Changing the master window does not automatically rewrite existing blocks; inspect the Vision timeline and block dates after making a change.

Export a backup

Choose Export backup in Settings or the signed-in page header. The browser downloads a JSON file containing the framework and records, including plans, targets, milestones, goals, tasks, habits and check-ins, daily records, reviews, journal entries, and archive entries. A backup can therefore contain sensitive personal information. Save it somewhere private, not in a publicly shared folder or repository.

Import a backup

Choose the JSON file in the import form and submit it. The app validates the data, leaves the source file unchanged, preserves existing personal records, and recognizes an exact file already imported to avoid duplicate imports. A complete Vision 30 backup may update matching framework plans and settings. Inspect older or legacy exports before importing; do not import data you do not want in this database.

Before an import

Export the current database first if you may need to recover its current state. An import is a database operation, not a preview. Legacy browser data is not synchronized automatically; the old dashboard must export a file that you then review and intentionally import.

Theme and account

The theme setting changes the app's visual palette. The account section identifies the signed-in username and points to Django admin for authorized account management. No public registration form is provided.

18 · EXAMPLE ROUTINES

Put the pages together without overloading the day

ABOUT 3 MINUTES

Morning orientation

  1. Open Today and check the date and active block.
  2. Set one to three priorities.
  3. Confirm which task is the next physical action.
  4. Choose the habits you intend to practice; do not pre-mark them.
AS WORK HAPPENS

Record only useful changes

  1. Mark a task complete when it is done.
  2. Record a habit after completing it.
  3. Update a goal or target only when you have a meaningful value or evidence.
  4. Reschedule tasks when the date no longer fits.
ABOUT 5 MINUTES

Evening close

  1. Complete any remaining pillar check-ins honestly.
  2. Write a win, lesson, or next adjustment if helpful.
  3. Save the daily reflection if you want a record or execution snapshot.
  4. Leave fields empty rather than inventing data.
ABOUT 15 MINUTES

Weekly reset

  1. Review tasks, goals, habits, and evidence.
  2. Open Reviews and assess the week.
  3. Carry forward only tasks that still matter.
  4. Choose a small set of next week's priorities.

These are suggestions, not required usage quotas. A shorter routine that fits your real life is better than a complex routine you cannot maintain.

19 · HELP AND GLOSSARY

Know what to do when something is unclear

A protected page sends me to sign in.

This is expected when signed out. Sign in with your account; Django normally returns you to the page you requested.

I do not have an account or forgot my password.

The app has no public registration or password reset form. Contact the site owner through a trusted channel. Never send them your current password.

A form showed an error after I saved.

Read the message at the top of the page and any field-level errors. Correct the highlighted dates or values, then submit again once. If it persists, tell the site owner the page and visible error text without sharing credentials or private backup contents.

Can I undo a delete?

The app's page-level delete controls do not provide an undo. Before removing important records, export a backup. Restore only from a backup you have reviewed and intend to import.

Why is there no score on an empty day?

The app does not treat a day with no saved score as a zero. Save an evening reflection on a day with measurable planned work if you want an execution snapshot.

Why does a goal still say in progress when its number reaches the target?

Numeric progress is not the same as verified completion. Review the goal's completion criteria and evidence, then enter a completion date only when the criteria have actually been met.

Does this app send reminders or sync old browser records?

No. There are no task reminder notifications, and the older static dashboard's browser storage is not automatically synced into this Django application. Use an inspected export file if you intentionally migrate legacy entries.

Execution
Recorded completion of planned actions and practices.
Outcome
A result or change assessed using a target, criteria, or evidence.
Evidence
A note or measure that supports an assessment; it should be specific enough to review later.
Block
A dated planning period with a vision, missions, milestones, and review.
Stage
A step in a pillar's longer development path, with its own plan and completion criteria.
Backup
A downloadable JSON copy of framework and personal database records. Keep it private.
